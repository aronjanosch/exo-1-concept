# Summary: The new wave of space games

Summary of a YouTube video on upcoming space games (https://youtu.be/WfxSQCZYloE). Private research note.

## Core thesis

A new wave of space games is moving away from the vast, empty sandbox to drift through. Instead they want you to **run something**: operate a corporation, command a warship, and manage every system aboard. The through line across all titles is **control, depth and management** — and the feeling that you should **feel the weight** of what you are doing.

## The four titles

### Space Reign
- Full 1.0 release in March, by Propulsive Games.
- You represent a private corporation in the near future; rival companies fight over resource-rich sectors.
- Layers several play styles into one interconnected experience: first-person cockpit fighters (rapid repeaters, railguns, weapons with a lot of weight) → third-person capital ship → RTS-style tactical view with ship-by-ship fleet orders.
- You are both commander and pilot, and the person deciding where the corporation invests and fights next. It threads a line through almost every activity in the setting.

### Dreadnought Tartarus — *the title that stands out most*
- Breathtaking **stylized** visuals (not "best graphics engine ever", but a deliberate stylized approach).
- Takes the operational thinking of Space Reign and focuses it into a **single colossal machine**: the city-sized battleship Tartarus.
- Strategic campaign map: plan routes, select priority targets, choose a safer path or push through a high-risk zone for greater reward.
- Modular ship: hull structures and armor plating, main cannons, long-range missiles, experimental super weapons, carrier-based fighters and support drones. Every decision directly shapes combat effectiveness, the ship's speed and its ability to dominate different encounter types.
- **Why it stands out:** the stylized look plus the concept of commanding one huge, constantly evolving ship.

### NTL: No Time Left
- 3D capital ship roguelike built on the foundation of FTL, reimagined as full 360° combat with Newtonian physics.
- You command a starship through a galaxy-spanning conspiracy.
- Simultaneously manage power distribution, shield allocation, weapon systems and support modules.
- Real time, but with a tactical pause: assess the situation, prioritize targets, line up broadsides, triage incoming damage, then unpause. Gives a rhythm of tension — bursts of chaos broken by moments of planning.
- **Environment affects everything:** magnetic fields disable unshielded systems, solar flares ignite fires you must fight alongside combat, black holes cause sustained damage with no direct defense. Forces you to adapt loadout and tactics.
- Ship progression lets you shape the ship toward your own play style.

### Solar Expanse
- Enters early access on April 9th. Applies the operational approach with **no weapons at all**; not a combat game, but high pressure and engaging.
- You lead an early 21st century space exploration company, reaching planets, moons and asteroids to mine and colonize them.
- Logistics are taken very seriously. Every celestial body is in constant motion (like the real solar system), so you cannot fly straight to a destination: account for orbital positions, optimize launch windows, factor in gravity assists. Wrong timing spirals costs (as in Kerbal Space Program); good timing gives a slingshot far beyond budget.
- Economic layer: compete with rival corporations for contracts and resources, balance flight costs against the value brought home; over time build permanent bases, fuel stations, launch sites, capsules, entire cities.
- Long-term: starts as near-future asteroid mining, spans centuries into terraforming, interstellar colony ships, and objectives like reaching Alpha Centauri and Trappist.

## Conclusion

Four games from different studios, countries and sub-genres. One blends cockpit combat with fleet command and corporate warfare; another focuses entirely on one colossal evolving battleship; the third turns space exploration into pure operational logistics without a single weapon; the fourth takes the roguelike format and fills it with deep systems and a real sense of consequence. What unites them — and signals the genre's new direction — is that they all want you to **feel the weight** of what you are doing.

**Why individual titles stand out:** Space Reign through layering multiple play styles; Dreadnought Tartarus through stylized visuals plus a single modular giant ship; NTL through its real-time/pause rhythm and environmental hazards; Solar Expanse through uncompromising orbital logistics with no weapons. The clearest "eyecatcher" in the video is **Dreadnought Tartarus**.
